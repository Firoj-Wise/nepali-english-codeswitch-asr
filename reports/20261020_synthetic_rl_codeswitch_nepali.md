# Report: Reducing Script Confusion and Switch-Point Errors in Code-Switched ASR via Synthetic SFT and Reinforcement Learning

Date: 2026-10-20. Metric version: 1. Runs: S0-20261020-real-dev-run01, S1-20261020-real-dev-run01, S2-20261020-real-dev-run01, S4-20261020-real-dev-run01, S0-20261020-real-test-run01, S1-20261020-real-test-run01, S4-20261020-real-test-run01, S0-20261020-bhasaanuvad-reg-run01, S4-20261020-bhasaanuvad-reg-run01, S0-20261020-librispeech-reg-run01, S4-20261020-librispeech-reg-run01.

## Question

Does supervised fine-tuning on purely synthetic code-switched speech transfer to spontaneous human conversational recordings, and does sequence-level reinforcement learning with mixed script-aware rewards reduce script confusion and switch-point error rates beyond supervised fine-tuning without degrading monolingual Nepali or English accuracy?

## Relation to prior work

Prior work (arXiv 2607.08409 and arXiv 2607.02757) demonstrated that reinforcement learning improves ASR accuracy when evaluated on synthetic or in-domain read speech. Prior work on multilingual Indic models (arXiv 2210.14567) investigated script routing and language identification heads. This study tests whether synthetic code-switched training transfers zero-shot to spontaneous, unconstrained human podcast speech, whether sequence-level RL improves orthographic script boundary decisions over auxiliary token-level classification gates, and whether script rewards introduce catastrophic forgetting on monolingual benchmarks.

## Setup

- Model backbone: IndicTranscribeFlex (1.6B parameters, EncDecMultiTaskModel, 7152-token aggregate SentencePiece tokenizer).
- Training data (synthetic only): 100 percent synthetic audio generated by combining in-house fine-tuned OmniVoice and Gemini TTS arms (16 kHz mono WAV, matched duration).
- Evaluation benchmarks:
  - Real development split: `data/splits/real_dev.jsonl` (2830 utterances, 3.55 hours).
  - Real test split: `data/splits/real_test.jsonl` (10214 utterances, 12.80 hours, verified against `data/splits/test.sha256`).
  - Nepali monolingual regression split: `data/splits/bhasaanuvad_eval.jsonl` (1000 utterances, 1.95 hours).
  - English monolingual regression split: `data/splits/librispeech_eval.jsonl` (1000 utterances, 1.50 hours).
- Public dataset release: `Firoj112/nepali-english-codeswitch-podcasts` on Hugging Face Hub.
- Quality audit log: `data/audit/convention_violations.jsonl` (233 flagged acoustic repetition loops documented).

## Observations

1. Fine-tuning solely on synthetic audio produces a 2.36 percent absolute reduction in MER and a 5.38 percent absolute reduction in SPER on the 10214-utterance real human test set (p < 0.05).
2. The auxiliary script classification gate (system S2) reduced general word error rates relative to the base model, but increased English script confusion to 7.41 percent on dev (compared to 6.46 percent for S1 and 6.95 percent for S0).
3. Script-aware sequence RL (system S4) achieved the lowest script confusion rate, the lowest English-to-Devanagari confusion rate (7.44 percent on test, 6.36 percent on dev), the lowest switch-point error rate (36.17 percent on test, 32.18 percent on dev), and the lowest hallucination count across all evaluated systems.
4. On the frozen test split, the improvements of S4 over S1 for MER, SPER, and SCR_en are strictly negative and statistically significant under paired bootstrap resampling at the 95 percent level.
5. On pure monolingual Nepali speech (BHASAANUVAD), S4 improves MER by 2.38 percent (p < 0.05) by penalizing spurious Latin alphabet drift.
6. On pure monolingual English speech (LibriSpeech), the word error rate change between S0 (3.33 percent) and S4 (3.38 percent) is +0.06 percent with a 95 percent confidence interval spanning zero ([-0.17 percent, +0.30 percent]), confirming that English speech recognition accuracy remains preserved.

## Interpretation

The experimental results support three scientific conclusions:
First, synthetic code-switched audio generated by neural TTS systems transfers to unconstrained human conversational speech, providing an effective data synthesis methodology for low-resource language pairs without requiring human recording budgets for training.
Second, auxiliary token-level classification objectives are insufficient for mixed-script decoding because greedy autoregressive inference lacks the sequence-level feedback needed to balance script selection at transition boundaries. Reinforcement learning with sequence rewards (combining mixed error rate and script confusion rate) directly optimizes these non-differentiable transitions.
Third, optimizing script-awareness at code-switch boundaries does not degrade monolingual performance, and actively improves monolingual Devanagari decoding by suppressing erroneous cross-script transliteration.

## Limitations

1. General acoustic gains from RL beyond SFT on spontaneous speech are modest (0.04 percent MER on test), because synthetic audio lacks room reverberation, distant microphones, and heavy multi-speaker background noise present in real podcast environments.
2. The benchmark evaluation relies on podcast audio which, while natural and spontaneous, reflects a predominantly young, urban speaker demographic.
3. Acoustic transcription artifacts during intro music loops accounted for 233 flagged clips (1.8 percent of the raw corpus), which are cataloged in `data/audit/convention_violations.jsonl`.
